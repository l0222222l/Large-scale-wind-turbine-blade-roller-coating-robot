The benchmark was conducted on representative side-surface regions extracted from the real STL model of a large wind-turbine blade. Dense line-scan profiles were simulated for each workstation, with approximately 800 ordered points in each profile and a deterministic controlled-error model including measurement noise, small workstation pose perturbation, and midpoint extraction bias. The NURBS and MLS baselines used all profile points to reconstruct or locally fit the surface before trajectory planning. In contrast, the proposed method directly extracted one midpoint from each line-scan profile and fitted the roller-coating trajectory without surface reconstruction. For 100 equivalent coating workstations, the proposed method reduced the processing time from 8.00 s for NURBS and 3.80 s for MLS to 0.34 s, corresponding to a 23.53-fold speed-up relative to NURBS. Although the proposed method produced larger trajectory deviations, its RMSE was 0.819 mm and its maximum error was 1.650 mm, remaining below the allowable roller-coating trajectory tolerance of 2.00 mm. These results indicate that, for large wind-turbine blade surfaces with smooth spanwise variation, full surface reconstruction is unnecessary for online coating trajectory acquisition.
